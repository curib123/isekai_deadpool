## Purpose

- introduce the amnesiac protagonist through immediate danger, not a slow orientation tour;
- establish the frontier as a dark-fantasy travel environment with roads, settlements, guilds, cursed wilderness, and a living archive route;
- make comedy a pressure response shared and rejected by different characters, not a constant stream of jokes;
- introduce the fantasy world and System rules through fights, escapes, inspections, injuries, and institutional decisions;
- begin the identity mystery while giving the Arc 1 local problem its own resolution.

## Environment and local-problem contract

- primary environment: frontier roads, settlements, cursed wilderness, guild offices, and an eastern archive route;
- movement rule: the environment must change at meaningful travel or action thresholds; a location cannot remain a static backdrop for several chapters;
- standalone local problem: a delivery and registry failure become a survival crisis for the frontier route and the people who depend on it;
- local antagonist: `UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED`; it may be a creature, route intelligence, corrupted institution, faction, or combination, but it must have a local motive and a resolvable local pressure;
- long-thread contribution: repeated System failures, the missing arrival, the moving route, and public suspicion that Red Jackal is impossible to classify;
- departure condition: the local route crisis is resolved or contained at a real cost, while the wider anomaly and Great Design questions remain open.

## Pacing contract

- every two or three chapters contain a physical danger, chase, fight, rescue, escape, or survival decision;
- every five-chapter movement contains one memorable action set piece with a clear objective, terrain, tactical problem, cost, and changed exit state;
- every chapter must move at least two of these: location, danger, knowledge, trust, resource, injury, public reputation, or objective;
- travel, guild procedure, and archive investigation must create immediate pressure rather than replace action;
- dark consequences remain visible after comedy: injuries, damaged property, fear, debt, grief, distrust, and institutional response;
- Red Jackal can solve any physical or narrative problem with his unrestricted ability, so suspense comes from his choices, relationships, restraint, curiosity, and willingness to let the world remain changed;
- existing Chapters 001-033 remain `UNCERTIFIED - author prose approval pending`; any mismatch between this action contract and their current prose requires a later locked-prose revision, not a silent canon claim.

## Six-movement Arc 1 spine

1. **The Summoning Breach — Chapters 001-005:** original-world death, broken arrival, and the first physical escape from a failed summoning space.
2. **The Valley Hunt — Chapters 006-010:** the first frontier travel danger turns Red Jackal's regeneration and imagination into visible survival choices.
3. **The Registry District Under Pressure — Chapters 011-015:** guild inspection becomes a public crisis when classification failure and local danger collide.
4. **The Guild Escort — Chapters 016-020:** a moving escort, pursuit, or route correction forces the group to protect one another while trust is still weak.
5. **The Unregistered Road — Chapters 021-025:** rumors, damage, and an active road threat make Red Jackal's status a danger to people who stand near him.
6. **The Moving Checkpoint — Chapters 026-030:** the first local travel gate tests the group and hands the sealed archive problem into Volume 02.
7. **The Archive That Eats Deliveries — Chapters 031-035:** the eastern archive turns a simple delivery into a living-record survival problem.
8. **The Underground Route — Chapters 036-040:** the group is chased through shifting archive passages and must choose between the easy exit and the dangerous truth.
9. **The Cursed Frontier — Chapters 041-045:** a new settlement or wilderness pocket faces a standalone curse or predator crisis connected to the route.
10. **The Broken Party — Chapters 046-050:** action separates the group, forcing cooperation, disagreement, and a costly rescue.
11. **The Beginner's Office in Danger — Chapters 051-055:** the local institution that sent Red Jackal becomes the target of the route crisis, turning bureaucracy into a defense problem.
12. **The First Impossible Survival — Chapters 056-060:** the local threat reaches its climax; Red Jackal survives an event that should kill a beginner, the frontier reacts, and the long road opens.
