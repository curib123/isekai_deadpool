# Prologue - You Want the Truth?

You want to know how all of this happened?

Fine. I'll tell you.

My name is Red Jackal.

It was not always Red Jackal. In fact, there was a long period when I had no idea what my name was supposed to be. There was another name buried somewhere behind the smoke in my memory, but every time I reached for it, my mind handed me a blank card and the emotional comfort of a locked filing cabinet.

So I chose Red Jackal.

It sounded better than “the unidentified man who keeps surviving things.” It was also shorter than the title the System wanted to give me, which was apparently nothing at all.

Before we begin, I should correct the opening.

I died.

That is the important part.

The first version of this story said I died heroically beneath a stormy sky while saving an innocent child from a speeding vehicle. It sounded impressive. It had weather. It had danger. It even had the kind of final image that makes people stare into the distance and whisper that I had potential.

I deleted that version.

The second version said I died because I was stupid.

That one was more accurate, but it lacked dignity.

The third version said I died because I was curious about what came after death.

I kept that one.

Not because it was the whole truth. It wasn't. I was also tired, distracted, carrying something I should not have been carrying, and entirely too confident that the universe understood my sense of humor.

The universe did not.

Or maybe it understood perfectly and decided to laugh first.

You will hear several versions of the truth as I tell this story. Some will be wrong because I misunderstood what was happening. Some will be incomplete because I do not want to discuss them yet. A few will change because I decide the sentence is not doing the moment justice.

That is not a mistake.

That is me telling the story.

You may think I am only talking to myself. You may think the reader is a habit, a coping mechanism, or a very patient imaginary friend.

You may think that until I decide you should think something else.

There. That sounded threatening. I did not mean it that way.

Probably.

The point is simple. I knew you were there from the beginning. I knew the story was a story. I knew I could change a line if I wanted to.

I also knew that changing everything immediately would make a terrible story.

And I had just died. I deserved to see what happened next.

So let us start with the day I died.

No heroic music. No grand prophecy. No mysterious voice promising me a better life.

Just rain, bad timing, a very specific kind of fan obsession, and one decision I could have rewritten.

I did not rewrite it.

That was my first mistake.

It was far from my last.
