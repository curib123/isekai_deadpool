# Chapter 002 - Red Jackal Tells You: The Summoning Error - Did I Open the Wrong Door?

“Then the summoning began.”

That was the line I gave you at the end of the previous chapter.

It sounded dramatic, so naturally the woman ruined it by staring at me like I had just crawled out of a drain.

“You changed what I said,” she told me.

“I improved what you said.”

“You changed it.”

“Improvement often looks like change to people with limited imagination.”

She looked at the red wall I had created in the middle of the road, the impossible status window beside me, and the fact that I had edited her greeting after dying.

“You are a problem.”

“That is the nicest thing anyone has said to me today.”

“You have been dead for less than a minute.”

“And already I am making an impression.”

She pressed two fingers against her temple. I understood the gesture. It meant either a headache or the sudden regret of accepting a job with unclear qualifications.

“Stop talking to the empty space.”

I glanced at you.

“She means you.”

“I know who I mean.”

“Then we have a communication problem.”

“The communication problem is you.”

“That is not a problem. That is a brand.”

The woman exhaled slowly.

The white room around us had no walls, no ceiling, and no floor that I could see. I was standing on something solid, but it had the personality of invisible glass. Every sound arrived too cleanly. My voice did not echo. Her voice did not echo. Even the silence seemed professionally arranged.

There was no rain. No pain. No broken ribs. No paramedic shouting instructions that arrived several seconds after they became useful.

I raised my arms and turned in a circle.

“Where am I?”

“Between places.”

“That sounds uncomfortable.”

“It is not a physical location.”

“So it is emotionally uncomfortable.”

“You are in a summoning chamber.”

“I was summoned?”

“You were received.”

“That sounds like a customer-service department.”

“You are not a customer.”

“The status window called me an anomaly. Pick a lane.”

She folded her hands in front of her white robe. Up close, the gold decorations were not decorations at all. They were thin symbols stitched into the cloth. Some moved when I looked at them. Others moved when I looked away.

I pointed at the nearest one.

“That symbol is blinking.”

“It is not.”

“It blinked again.”

“You are seeing things.”

“I died five minutes ago. I am allowed to see things.”

She stared at me.

“How do you know how long it has been?”

“I don't.”

“Then why did you say five minutes?”

“It sounded specific.”

“You are impossible to question.”

“Thank you. I work hard.”

The woman made a small movement with her hand. The status window rotated until it faced her.

“Your identity cannot be confirmed.”

“I told you. Deadpool.”

“That is not an identity.”

“It is a very popular one.”

“It is not in the registry.”

“Your registry needs better entertainment.”

“The registry records souls, not fictional characters.”

The room became quieter.

I looked at her.

“Fictional?”

“Yes.”

“You know what fiction is?”

“Of course.”

“You know what a movie is?”

“I know what an illusion is.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It is close enough for this conversation.”

“No, it is really not.”

I turned toward you.

“This is the part where the story puts a hand on my shoulder and whispers that everything I believed was a lie.”

The woman frowned. “What story?”

“Exactly.”

“That was not an answer.”

“It was a better answer than the question deserved.”

She moved her fingers across the air. Lines of light appeared beneath the status window. They formed circles, then boxes, then symbols that looked like someone had dropped a dictionary down a staircase.

“You are an Earth-born human.”

“Correct.”

“Your body is not the body you had before death.”

“Also correct.”

“Your soul has no complete registration.”

“Rude, but correct.”

“Your memories are damaged.”

I stopped smiling.

The woman noticed.

Of course she noticed. People always noticed when the joke stopped before reaching the punchline. They just pretended they did not because admitting it made the room heavier.

“How damaged?” I asked.

“Your personal identity is fragmented.”

“Meaning?”

“Name unknown. Family records inaccessible. Childhood records inaccessible. Employment records inaccessible. Physical appearance unstable in memory. Emotional associations incomplete.”

She said it like she was reading a report.

I hated reports.

“What do I remember?”

“You remember a fictional man.”

“He is not fictional.”

“The registry says he is.”

“The registry also says I am undefined. It is having a difficult day.”

She ignored that.

“You remember his name. His appearance. His combat style. His weapons. His regeneration. His humor. His habit of addressing an audience that does not exist.”

I raised one finger.

“First, rude. Second, the audience exists.”

“There is no one there.”

I pointed at you again.

“That is what you think.”

“There is no one there.”

“You keep saying that like confidence can replace observation.”

“You are speaking to empty space.”

“I am speaking to people who have excellent patience.”

“There are no people.”

“You are being very judgmental about my friends.”

For the first time, the woman's expression changed from irritation to concern.

I did not like that expression.

Concern was worse than anger. Anger meant someone was willing to fight. Concern meant they had already decided you were a damaged animal near a sharp object.

“Do not look at me like that,” I said.

“Like what?”

“Like I need supervision.”

“You need supervision.”

“I need a tour guide.”

“You edited my speech.”

“I edited one sentence.”

“You created a physical wall from imagination.”

“A tasteful wall.”

“You allowed yourself to be killed.”

“That was a personal choice.”

“You are talking to an invisible audience.”

“They are very real to me.”

“You just said they were real.”

“I contain multitudes.”

The woman looked toward the status window again.

“The System has classified you as an anomaly.”

“Finally. A professional opinion.”

“It does not mean what you think it means.”

“It means I am special.”

“It means the world does not know how to process your existence.”

“That also means I am special.”

“It means you are dangerous.”

“That is just special with better advertising.”

She closed her hand.

The status window vanished.

“You cannot keep changing the conversation.”

“I can.”

“You should not.”

“That is a different argument.”

“You could destabilize the summoning chamber.”

“Could I?”

“Yes.”

“Would it be funny?”

“No.”

“Then I probably shouldn't.”

She stared at me.

“You are choosing not to?”

“I am choosing not to do something because it would be boring. That is different from being unable.”

The woman studied me for a long moment.

“What do you want?”

That was a dangerous question.

Most people asked what I wanted when they expected an answer like money, revenge, food, or a dramatic sword. Nobody expected the honest answer.

I wanted to remember my name.

I wanted to remember the faces that had vanished from my mind.

I wanted to know why Deadpool was the only person my memory had protected.

I wanted to know why I could change the shape of a scene but could not reach through the locked door inside my own head.

I wanted to know if the reader was real.

I wanted to know whether any of those questions mattered.

So I gave her the answer she could survive.

“I want a sword.”

The woman looked tired.

“Why?”

“Because I am about to enter a medieval fantasy world.”

“You do not know that.”

“You are wearing a robe, standing in a white void, and using a magical status window. If this is not a medieval fantasy world, somebody has made a serious branding error.”

“It is a world of magic.”

“And monsters?”

“Yes.”

“Demons?”

“Yes.”

“Kings, adventurers, ancient ruins, and suspiciously attractive people with tragic backstories?”

She hesitated.

“Some of those exist.”

“Excellent. I am prepared.”

“You have no weapon.”

“I have imagination.”

“That is not a weapon.”

I imagined a sword in my hand.

Reality gave me a wooden spoon.

I looked down at it.

The woman looked down at it.

You looked down at it.

“That was not what I imagined,” I said.

“You imagined a sword.”

“Yes.”

“And the world produced a spoon.”

“The world is making a joke.”

“Perhaps your ability is less reliable than you believe.”

I turned the spoon around in my hand. It was perfectly ordinary, except for the tiny gold crown engraved near the handle.

I imagined the spoon becoming a sword.

It became a sword.

The woman went silent.

“That was different,” I said. “The first result was a punchline.”

“You could have changed it at any time.”

“Yes.”

“Why did you let the spoon appear?”

“Because the joke was good.”

She looked at the sword. It had a red blade, a black handle, and a guard shaped like two crooked grins.

“You are not taking this seriously.”

“I am taking it exactly as seriously as it deserves.”

“You are about to enter a dangerous world.”

“Then give me the tour before something tries to eat me.”

The woman raised both hands.

Two doors appeared behind her.

The first was tall and gold, covered in glowing symbols.

The second was small, gray, and labeled **AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY**.

“The gold door leads to the mortal world,” she said. “The gray door is a maintenance passage.”

“Why is the maintenance passage more interesting?”

“It is not.”

“It has a sign.”

“The sign is a warning.”

“Warnings are invitations written by nervous people.”

“Do not open it.”

I looked at the gold door.

I looked at the gray door.

I looked at you.

“We are obviously opening the gray door.”

The woman moved faster than I expected. She reached for my shoulder.

I edited the scene.

Her hand missed me by three inches.

The gray door opened.

Cold wind rushed through the room. Beyond it was darkness, a distant red glow, and the sound of something enormous breathing.

The woman went pale.

“That door is not supposed to open.”

“You should put that on the sign.”

The breathing stopped.

Something on the other side noticed us.

I tightened my grip on the sword.

The woman grabbed the back of my coat.

“Close it.”

“I could.”

“Then close it.”

“But we just met.”

“This is not the time for social development.”

“You are right. We should save that for after the monster attack.”

The darkness behind the gray door moved.

I smiled at you.

“Good news. I found the wrong door.”

The woman shouted something in a language I did not understand.

The thing behind the door answered.

Then the summoning chamber lost its lights.
